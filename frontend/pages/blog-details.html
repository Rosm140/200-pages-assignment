10 Things to Check Before Buying a Flat in 2024

Priya Sharma
Senior Property Consultant
•
November 12, 2024
•
14,280 views

Buying a flat is one of the biggest financial decisions of your life. With thousands of options available across India's booming real estate market, it's easy to get swept up in the excitement and overlook critical details that could cost you significantly later.

After helping over 3,000 families find their perfect home, our experts at HomeQuest have compiled the definitive checklist for flat buyers in 2024.

💡 Quick Summary: Before signing any agreement, verify RERA registration, title documents, builder track record, legal clearances, construction quality, hidden charges, amenities, resale value, loan eligibility, and possession timeline.

1. Verify RERA Registration

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, mandates that all residential projects above a certain size be registered with the state RERA authority. Always verify the project's RERA registration number on your state's official RERA website before making any payment.

A RERA-registered project guarantees that the builder has disclosed all project details — carpet area, completion timeline, financial disclosures — to the regulatory authority. It also gives you legal recourse in case of delays or disputes.

2. Check the Builder's Track Record

Research the developer's history extensively. Look for:

  • Number of projects completed on time
  • Quality of past constructions (visit completed projects)
  • Any legal disputes or complaints filed with RERA
  • Financial stability and funding sources
  • Customer reviews on platforms like HomeQuest and Google

3. Verify Title Documents

A clean title is non-negotiable. Engage a property lawyer to verify:

  • Title deed and ownership chain for the past 30 years
  • Encumbrance certificate (EC) showing no loans or liabilities
  • Land use approvals from local authorities
  • No objection certificates (NOCs) from relevant departments
"Never skip the title verification step. We've seen families lose their entire investment because of pre-existing legal disputes that weren't discovered until after registration." – Vikram Joshi, Co-Founder, HomeQuest

4. Understand the Carpet Area vs. Super Built-Up Area

Post-RERA, builders must quote prices based on carpet area — the actual usable floor area within the walls of your flat. The super built-up area (which includes common areas, staircase, etc.) is typically 25-40% higher than carpet area. Always clarify which area calculation is being used in your agreement.

5. Check for Hidden Charges

The advertised price is rarely the final price. Watch out for:

  • Preferential Location Charges (PLC) for higher floors or corner units
  • Club house and amenity charges
  • Parking charges (covered vs. open)
  • Power backup charges
  • Stamp duty and registration fees (5-7% of property value)
  • GST on under-construction properties (5% for regular, 1% for affordable housing)

6. Inspect Construction Quality

If possible, visit the construction site or completed units in the same project. Check:

  • Quality of concrete, bricks, and finishing materials
  • Plumbing and electrical specifications
  • Quality of fittings — tiles, sanitary ware, windows, doors
  • Waterproofing of bathrooms and terraces

7. Evaluate Amenities and Infrastructure

Check what amenities are promised vs. what's legally committed in the agreement. Common discrepancies include gym facilities, swimming pool dimensions, power backup coverage, and landscaping. Only amenities mentioned in the sale agreement are legally enforceable.

8. Assess Connectivity and Neighbourhood

A home isn't just its four walls. Evaluate proximity to:

  • Workplace and public transport (metro, bus)
  • Schools and hospitals
  • Shopping centres and markets
  • Upcoming infrastructure projects that could increase value

9. Check Home Loan Eligibility

Get a loan pre-approval before finalising a property. This gives you a clear budget and speeds up the purchase process. Note that banks have their own technical and legal evaluation process — a project that passes your checks may still be rejected by certain banks if there are compliance issues.

10. Confirm the Possession Timeline

The agreement must clearly state the possession date with a penalty clause for delays. Under RERA, builders must pay interest at SBI's marginal cost-based lending rate (MCLR) + 2% for every month of delay. Ensure this clause is in your agreement.

✅ Final Checklist:
  • RERA registration verified
  • Builder track record checked
  • Title documents verified by lawyer
  • Carpet area calculation confirmed
  • All charges accounted for in budget
  • Construction quality inspected
  • Amenities confirmed in agreement
  • Connectivity assessed personally
  • Loan pre-approval obtained
  • Possession timeline with penalty clause
